# Daily streak, and one shared card pass after every task

The app navigation now carries a flame, a streak length, and a small `+N` badge of *saved days*.
Two pieces of work sit behind it: a change to when new flashcards become due and a card pass that
both task types now share, and the streak itself.

## What a learner sees

**In the navigation.** A flame with a number. The flame is lit once today counts, dim before that,
and out when there is no streak. A small superscript badge shows saved days when there are any; at
zero nothing is drawn. Clicking opens a short card explaining why today does or does not count, how
many cards are still waiting in which language, and what saved days are for.

**Making a day count.** Two things, in either order: finish a quest (a conversation or a
translation), and empty the review queue. Both stick for the rest of the day, so cards falling due
in the evening never take back a day that already counted.

**Saved days.** Doing more than the minimum banks spare days, with diminishing returns: three
quests in one day earn the first, six the second, ten the third, and three is the maximum. Earning
them ignores the review half of the rule — three quests bank a day even if the cards were never
touched, though the day itself still stays dark.

**Missing a day.** Each missed day spends one saved day and still adds one to the number, so a
12-day streak with two saved days comes back from a two-day absence reading 14 with an empty badge.
When the badge cannot cover everything missed, the streak ends and starts again at 1. The number
therefore means "days your streak has survived", not "days you studied" — the Archive remains the
honest record of actual work.

**A card pass after every task.** Translation already ended with a short pass over the vocabulary it
produced. Conversation feedback now offers the same thing: a button at the end of the page starts a
pass over the notes collected there. Both use the same cards, the same Incorrect/Pass buttons, and
the same queue behaviour (Incorrect sends a card to the back with a different example; Pass removes
it).

**New notes wait a day.** A note created by finishing a task no longer joins the same day's review
queue. The task that produced it already drills it in its own pass; spaced repetition starts the
next day. This is also what makes the streak's second condition reachable in one sitting — before
it, finishing a task refilled the queue at the exact moment the streak looked at it.

## How it works

**The rules are a pure module.** `src/lib/streak.ts` holds every rule — settling missed days,
earning saved days, lighting a day — as plain functions that take today's date as an argument and
read no clock. The server, the browser, the tests and the development harness all run the same
code, so none of them can disagree about what day it is or what a day is worth.

**Nothing is written when a page is read.** Settling missed days is derived from the stored row and
today's date every time anyone looks. Only two kinds of request write: a quest completing and a card
rating. This matters because SvelteKit can load a page speculatively when a link is hovered; a
loader that wrote would quietly burn saved days.

**One row per learner** (`user_streak`), holding the streak length, the last day it counted through,
the saved days, and today's counters. Counters dated to an earlier day read as zero, so there is no
nightly reset job. Existing learners start at zero: past review-queue state cannot be reconstructed,
so there is no backfill.

**Crediting a quest exactly once** relies on the fact that every completion path already claims its
transition under a lock or a conditional update. The credit joins that same transaction, so a claim
that loses the race credits nothing. It runs inside a savepoint and its failures are logged and
swallowed: a streak counter must never roll back a finished conversation. The trade is that a
swallowed failure is a lost count, not a retried one — acceptable for a counter, and written down
rather than glossed over.

**Checking the review queue** is one query across every language, since the streak is account-wide
while `/review` is per-language. It compares stored dates as text rather than casting them, so one
corrupt card cannot abort a quest completion; a test pins it against the rule `/review` itself uses.

**Animations fire off a remembered view, not off the previous render.** Finishing a conversation
navigates with a full page load, so the indicator arrives with nothing to compare against. Instead
each device remembers the last streak it acknowledged and plays whatever explains the difference.
That also stops the "your streak ended" notice from reappearing on every page until the next write.
A timer to local midnight, plus a check when the tab becomes visible again, fades a lit flame back
to waiting without a round trip.

## Testing it

`/streak-lab` (development only) has four panels: one drives the real navigation indicator into any
state, one replays each animation at up to four times slower with a forced reduced-motion toggle,
one runs an editable day-by-day timeline through the rules with no database at all, and one performs
real writes through the production functions. It also travels in time: a development cookie shifts
what the server and the browser both consider "today", so "come back tomorrow, then the day after"
is testable end to end without touching the system clock.

## Things worth knowing

- The review half of the daily rule can be satisfied by deleting notes. There is no leaderboard and
  no sharing, so the only person a learner can mislead is themselves; guarding it was judged not
  worth the machinery.
- A conversation counts as a quest when the conversation ends, not when its card pass ends. A
  translation counts after its pass only because that pass is part of the attempt itself. The
  asymmetry is intentional: credit should land where the learner feels finished.
- The timezone comes from the browser and is therefore learner-controlled. Day arithmetic never
  moves backwards, so a changed timezone cannot re-open a day that already counted. Moving forwards
  works, but it only skips days — which spends saved days and can end a streak. No path invents a
  day that was neither worked for nor paid for.
- A pass resumed the day after its notes were created is drilling cards that are genuinely due that
  day, so they stay in the queue until they graduate. That is correct, not a gap.
