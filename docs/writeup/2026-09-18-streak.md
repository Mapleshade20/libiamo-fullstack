# Daily streak, and one shared card pass after every task

The homepage now carries a flame, a streak length, and a small `+N` badge of *saved days*. Two
pieces of work sit behind it: a change to when new flashcards become due and a card pass that both
task types now share, and the streak itself.

## What a learner sees

**On the homepage.** A drawn flame with a number beside it. The flame is a cold gray silhouette
while neither half of the daily rule is met, gains a small blue kindling flame when one half is, and
burns fully when both are. A small superscript badge shows saved days when there are any; at zero
nothing is drawn.

**The clipboard.** Clicking the flame slides a wooden clipboard up from the bottom of the screen: a
month of the learner's actual history, today's two conditions with their state, how many cards are
still waiting in which language, and what saved days are for. With a two-part daily rule this is how
someone finds out why today is still dark. The month is a real calendar — it pages backwards, marks
worked days and days a saved day covered with different marks, and joins consecutive days into a
band, so a run is visible as a shape rather than as a number.

**The reward.** Finishing the thing that completes a day brings a small notice down from the top of
the screen, which waits for the flame to finish igniting, shows the week, and leaves on its own. It
never takes focus and never has to be dismissed, and it arrives at the end of the task rather than
in the middle of it — after a translation's summary, after a review session's summary, at the
feedback page of a conversation.

**Making a day count.** Two things, in either order: finish a quest (a conversation or a
translation), and empty the review queue. Both stick for the rest of the day, so cards falling due
in the evening never take back a day that already counted.

**Saved days.** Doing more than the minimum banks spare days, with diminishing returns: three
quests in one day earn the first, six the second, ten the third, and three is the maximum. Earning
them ignores the review half of the rule — three quests bank a day even if the cards were never
touched, though the day itself still stays dark.

**Missing a day.** Each missed day spends one saved day and still adds one to the number, so a
12-day streak with two saved days comes back from a two-day absence reading 14 with an empty badge.
When the badge cannot cover everything missed, the streak ends and starts again at 1. The number
therefore means "days your streak has survived", not "days you studied" — the Archive remains the
honest record of actual work.

**A card pass after every task.** Translation already ended with a short pass over the vocabulary it
produced. Conversation feedback now offers the same thing: a button at the end of the page starts a
pass over the notes collected there. Both use the same cards, the same Incorrect/Pass buttons, and
the same queue behaviour (Incorrect sends a card to the back with a different example; Pass removes
it).

**New notes wait a day.** A note created by finishing a task no longer joins the same day's review
queue. The task that produced it already drills it in its own pass; spaced repetition starts the
next day. This is also what makes the streak's second condition reachable in one sitting — before
it, finishing a task refilled the queue at the exact moment the streak looked at it.

## How it works

**The rules are a pure module.** `src/lib/streak.ts` holds every rule — settling missed days,
earning saved days, lighting a day — as plain functions that take today's date as an argument and
read no clock. The server, the browser, the tests and the development harness all run the same
code, so none of them can disagree about what day it is or what a day is worth.

**Nothing is written when a page is read.** Settling missed days is derived from the stored row and
today's date every time anyone looks. Only three kinds of request write: a quest completing, a card
rating, and a visible page confirming an empty queue (`POST /api/streak/observe`, credited
silently). This matters because SvelteKit can load a page speculatively when a link is hovered; a
loader that wrote would quietly burn saved days.

**One row per learner** (`user_streak`), holding the streak length, the last day it counted through,
the saved days, and today's counters. Counters dated to an earlier day read as zero, so there is no
nightly reset job. Existing learners start at zero: past review-queue state cannot be reconstructed,
so there is no backfill.

**The calendar reads real days, not the streak number.** That one row answers "how long" in a single
lookup but cannot answer "which days", and a calendar reconstructed from the number would be a
fiction: it cannot tell a day that was worked from a day a saved day paid for. So a second, sparse
table records one row per day that actually had an outcome — nothing for inactive days, no event
log — written in the same transaction and under the same lock as the row it sits beside. The
first version of the clipboard showed a seven-day strip extrapolated from the count and had to be
labelled "continuity retained" rather than "studied"; it now shows what happened.

**Days before the records begin are drawn as unknown, not as failures.** Each learner carries a
boundary date marking where continuous recording starts. Below it, a missing day means "not
recorded"; above it, a missing day means "not studied". Existing learners got that boundary at the
migration's local date, so the months before the feature existed read as blank rather than as a wall
of missed days. A day that *is* recorded still shows, even below the boundary — the boundary governs
absences only.

**Crediting a quest exactly once** relies on the fact that every completion path already claims its
transition under a lock or a conditional update. The credit joins that same transaction, so a claim
that loses the race credits nothing. It runs inside a savepoint and its failures are logged and
swallowed: a streak counter must never roll back a finished conversation. The trade is that a
swallowed failure is a lost count, not a retried one — acceptable for a counter, and written down
rather than glossed over.

**Checking the review queue** is one query across every language, since the streak is account-wide
while `/review` is per-language. It compares stored dates as text rather than casting them, so one
corrupt card cannot abort a quest completion; a test pins it against the rule `/review` itself uses.

**Rewards fire off a remembered receipt, not off the previous render.** Finishing a conversation
navigates with a full page load, so the flame arrives with nothing to compare against. Instead each
tab remembers the progress it last acknowledged and plays whatever explains the difference. A first
visit and a new day set that memory silently; extra quests do not replay a reward; a stale, lower
record cannot undo one already acknowledged. A pending reward survives the reload and waits for the
task to reach its own ending before it appears. A timer to local midnight, plus a check when the tab
becomes visible again, fades a lit flame back to waiting without a round trip.

**The artwork is swappable.** The flame is a component behind a small interface — appearance,
ignite, moving, reduced motion, and a signal for "ignition finished". A seasonal skin can change the
drawing and the length of its ignition without touching the streak rules, the receipts, the calendar
or the server; the reward waits for that signal rather than counting frames.

## Testing it

`/streak-lab` (development only) drives the real homepage flame, the real receipt logic and the real
settlement gate rather than a gallery of hand-posed states — a gallery drifts from the components it
illustrates. Its buttons complete quests and reviews in either order, earn saved days, travel to the
next day, slow the motion down and force reduced motion. Its receipts live in memory and none of its
controls write the account. A separate panel runs an editable day-by-day timeline through the rules
with no database at all. Time travel is a development cookie that shifts what the server and the
browser both consider "today", so "come back tomorrow, then the day after" is testable end to end
without touching the system clock.

Verified with `pnpm check`, the unit suite and a production build. Beyond those, one-off checks were
run against the isolated development database and a live browser and then discarded rather than kept
as scripts, since neither could be re-run without tooling outside the repo: a rolled-back fixture
confirmed the daily table's round-trip, duplicate safety, state constraint, range query and cascade;
a driven `/streak-lab` on desktop and at 390px confirmed both completion orders, the drawn paths,
duplicate suppression, the late calendar reveal, modal focus and scroll restoration, reduced motion
and the exact static return after an idle cycle. The calendar endpoint answered 200 with
`private, no-store` when authenticated and 400 for an invalid month.

The migration was applied to the isolated `localhost/libiamo_a`; `libiamo_db` was not modified. No
real quest or review was submitted during any of this.

## Things worth knowing

- The review half of the daily rule can be satisfied by deleting notes. There is no leaderboard and
  no sharing, so the only person a learner can mislead is themselves; guarding it was judged not
  worth the machinery.
- A conversation counts as a quest when the conversation ends, not when its card pass ends. A
  translation counts after its pass only because that pass is part of the attempt itself. The
  asymmetry is intentional: credit should land where the learner feels finished.
- The timezone comes from the browser and is therefore learner-controlled. Day arithmetic never
  moves backwards, so a changed timezone cannot re-open a day that already counted. Moving forwards
  works, but it only skips days — which spends saved days and can end a streak. No path invents a
  day that was neither worked for nor paid for.
- A pass resumed the day after its notes were created is drilling cards that are genuinely due that
  day, so they stay in the queue until they graduate. That is correct, not a gap.
- Daily history grows by at most one row per active day and is never trimmed today. If it ever needs
  a limit, the design records where that belongs and the one rule it has to obey: trimming must move
  the "records begin here" boundary forward at the same time, or the deleted days would come back as
  missed days rather than as unknown ones.
