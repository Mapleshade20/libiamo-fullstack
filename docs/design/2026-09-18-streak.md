# Daily streak with saved days

A Duolingo-style streak lives in the app navigation: a flame, the streak length, and a small
superscript badge holding *saved days* earned by doing more than the daily minimum. Saved days are
spent automatically to cover days the learner misses, so a streak only dies when the badge is empty.

**Depends on `docs/design/2026-09-18-note-transfer-unification.md`.** That design makes a new note
due from the learner's next local day; without it the notes a task just produced keep the review
queue non-empty at the exact moment this feature checks it, and the review gate below is
unsatisfiable in one sitting.

## 1. Rules

### 1.1 What lights a day

A day counts as **lit** when both of these have been observed during that local day:

1. the learner completed at least one **quest** — a practice session reaching `completed`, or a
   translation attempt reaching `completed`; and
2. their **review queue was empty** — at an observation point during the day, no Note of theirs was
   available for study, using the same availability rule `/review` uses (`due <= now`, plus
   Learning/Relearning cards inside the 20-minute learn-ahead window).

Both are sticky within the day and order does not matter: clearing reviews at 09:00 then finishing a
quest at 21:00 lights the day exactly as the reverse does.

The gate is a **positive observation, never a retraction.** "Empty" is checked at two points — after
each Note rating, and when a quest completes — and `reviewCleared` is only ever set to true. Cards
falling due later in the day do not darken a day that was already lit; this is what makes the rule
stable, and it is the only reading consistent with the sticky requirement.

The gate is account-wide while `/review` is per-language (`getDueNotes(userId, language, …)`), so a
learner studying two languages must clear both. Nothing in the repo counts available cards across
languages today — `getReviewStats` and `/api/review/stats` are both scoped to
`user.activeLanguage` — so the breakdown the popover shows is a new capability, specified in §4.3
rather than assumed.

A quest is credited when the conversation or attempt completes, which is the same predicate Archive
already treats as a finished activity. The practice transfer stage added by the dependency design
does **not** move that moment: credit lands where the learner feels they finished the task, and the
card pass is rewarded through the review gate instead. Translation is credited after its transfer
only because that stage sits inside the attempt's own lifecycle; the asymmetry is deliberate.

Clearing reviews gates *lighting the day*. It has no effect on earning saved days (§1.2), and
clearing reviews is not itself a unit of work — only quests count toward the task count.

> Accepted risk: the gate is satisfiable by deleting notes in `/review/manage`. The streak has no
> social or competitive surface, so the only person a learner deceives is themselves. Not worth
> anti-cheat machinery; recorded here so it is a decision rather than an oversight.

### 1.2 Streak length and saved days

- The first lit day sets the streak to 1. Each further lit day adds 1.
- Quests earn **saved days** with diminishing returns, by cumulative count within the local day:

  | quests that day | saved days earned |
  | --------------- | ----------------- |
  | 1–2             | 0                 |
  | 3–5             | +1                |
  | 6–9             | +2                |
  | 10+             | +3 (the cap)      |

  Thresholds are the triangular numbers 1, 3, 6, 10 …: after the first quest, 2 more earn the first
  saved day, 3 more the second, 4 more the third.
- **Earning ignores the review gate.** Three quests grant +1 even if reviews were never cleared; the
  day's main number still does not light. Lighting and earning are independent outcomes of the same
  day.
- Saved days are capped at **3** (`STREAK_BANK_MAX`). `savedDaysFor(n)` returns the capped value, so
  a fifteen-quest day yields 3 and never a number the column's check constraint rejects. When the
  badge is full, extra quests earn nothing and the popover says so instead of showing a next target.
- A badge of 0 is not rendered at all — no `+0`, no empty pill, no reserved gap.

### 1.3 Missing days

Settlement covers days that have *fully elapsed* without being lit. Today is never settled while it
is still running.

- 0 missed days: the streak is alive. Before today is lit it shows **pending** — flame and main
  number dimmed, the saved-days badge keeping its normal colour and value.
- `missed <= saved days`: each missed day spends one saved day and **adds one to the main number**.
  A 12-day streak with 2 saved days, returning after a 2-day absence, shows 14 and no badge.
- `missed > saved days`: the streak is over. Main number 0, flame out, carried saved days gone. The
  next lit day starts again at 1.

Covered days counting toward the main number is a deliberate product choice (it is what the feature
was asked for): the number reads as "days your streak has survived", not "days you studied". The
Archive remains the honest record of actual work, and the popover distinguishes the two.

Two consequences of earning being independent of the gate:

- A saved day earned on a dark day can cover that same day once it elapses. Doing three quests
  without touching reviews continues the streak and empties one badge slot — the intended trade,
  not a loophole.
- Saved days can exist with no streak (quests done after a break, on a day that stays dark). The
  badge shows them, the main number stays 0, and they are spent by the next absence once a streak
  restarts. Settlement only consumes days carried in from earlier days; saved days earned *today*
  are never taken by today's settlement.

### 1.4 Days and time zones

The streak day is the learner's **local calendar day**, from the validated browser-timezone cookie
behind `displayClock` (`src/lib/server/browser-timezone.ts`). Days are plain `YYYY-MM-DD` strings;
differences are computed on UTC-parsed dates so DST never shortens or lengthens a day.

The cookie is learner-controlled, so day arithmetic is clamped to be monotonic: the effective today
is `max(computedToday, throughDate, progressDate)`. Both stored dates matter — a covered day
advances `through_date` while `progress_date` can sit further back, so clamping on one alone leaves
a gap.

The clamp only blocks moving *backwards*. A forward jump (UTC−12 to UTC+14 is 26 hours of headroom)
still advances the calendar, and the days it skips settle exactly as a real absence does: saved days
are spent, and the streak breaks when they run out. So a learner can burn their own badge faster,
but they cannot light a day without completing a quest on it, and no path adds streak days that were
not either worked for or paid for with saved days.

## 2. Persistence

### 2.1 Settlement is derived, never written by a loader

Settlement (§1.3) is a **pure function of the stored row and today's date**, so every reader derives
it and no `GET` ever writes. Rows change only on two POST paths: a quest completing and a Note
rating. This avoids the write-on-read hazard the unread-receipt code warns about and keeps preloaded
layout loads harmless.

Every write starts by materialising settlement through yesterday, then applies the day's own effects
(saved days earned, and lighting the day if both conditions hold). Materialising is safe because
`through_date` advances to the last day counted — lit or covered — so re-running `settle` on the
written row gives the same answer. Derivation and materialisation agree, and a row untouched since
an absence still displays correctly.

### 2.2 Schema

One row per user, created on first progress (`user_streak`, in `auth.schema.ts` next to `userQuota`,
since it hangs off the user rather than the learning content):

| column               | type      | meaning                                                      |
| -------------------- | --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `user_id`            | text PK   | FK `user.id`, `on delete cascade`                             |
| `streak_days`        | int       | days counted through `through_date`                           |
| `through_date`       | date null | last day counted into `streak_days`, lit **or** covered by a saved day; null exactly when `streak_days = 0` |
| `bank`               | int       | saved days, `0..3`, including any earned today                |
| `progress_date`      | date null | the local day the three counters below describe               |
| `task_count`         | int       | quests completed on `progress_date`                           |
| `review_cleared`     | boolean   | the queue was observed empty on `progress_date`               |
| `bank_earned_today`  | int       | saved days already granted for `progress_date`, making repeat grants exact-once |
| `time_zone`          | text null | zone used at the last write, for diagnosis only               |
| `updated_at`         | timestamp |                                                               |

Checks: `streak_days >= 0`, `bank between 0 and 3`, `bank_earned_today between 0 and 3`,
`task_count >= 0`, `(streak_days = 0) = (through_date is null)`. Saved days may exist without a
streak (§1.3), so there is deliberately no constraint tying `bank` to `streak_days`.

Counters whose `progress_date` is not today read as zero, so no nightly reset job is needed.

No backfill. Historical review-queue state cannot be reconstructed, so every existing learner starts
at 0 on the day the feature ships; a missing row reads as the empty state.

### 2.3 Where writes happen

`src/lib/server/streak.ts` owns all writes:

```ts
recordQuestCompletion(tx: Transaction, userId: string, at: Date, timeZone: string): Promise<void>
recordReviewObservation(userId: string, at: Date, timeZone: string): Promise<StreakRecord | null>
```

**Invariant, and the whole reason this needs no idempotency key: `recordQuestCompletion` may only be
called from the transaction that won the state-machine claim for that completion.** Every call site
already fences its transition, so a business event can reach the helper at most once:

| call site | fence |
| --------- | ----- |
| `session.ts:593` (user-requested completion) | `for update` + `status === "in_progress"` inside the transaction |
| `session.ts:444` (max-turns completion) | same guarded transition |
| `translation-practice.ts` `completeTranslationTransfer` | `where(workflowPhase = 'transfer')` + `returning` |
| `translation-workflow.ts:296` `finishTranslationCorrections`, no-cards branch | `where(workflowPhase = 'correction')` + `returning` |

That is the complete list: `agent-replies/worker.ts` only ever moves a session to `abandoned` or
`cancelled`, never `completed`, so it is not an entry point. The two translation calls are **not in
transactions today** (`db.update` directly) — transactionalising them is part of this work, not an
afterthought.

Consequences that must be written into the implementation:

- **Savepoint isolation.** A streak failure must not roll back the quest. `recordQuestCompletion`
  wraps its own work in `tx.transaction()`, which drizzle's postgres-js driver implements as a
  savepoint, and the caller logs and swallows. Swallowing without the savepoint aborts the outer
  transaction, so the two are one decision, not two. `recordReviewObservation` runs outside any
  caller transaction and simply logs on failure.
- **No implicit retry.** There is no event log and no completion receipt, so a swallowed failure is
  a permanently lost quest count for that day — not something "picked up next time". This is the
  price of having no event table, and it is acceptable for a counter, but the design must not
  pretend otherwise.
- **Create then lock.** `SELECT … FOR UPDATE` cannot lock a row that does not exist, so both helpers
  do `insert … on conflict (user_id) do nothing` first, then lock and read.
- `recordReviewObservation` runs **after `rateNote` returns**, at both rating entry points — the API
  route `src/routes/api/review/[noteId]/rate/+server.ts` and the shared `rateTransferNote`. Those
  two are the complete set of rating paths, and both have request context, which is where the
  timezone comes from (`getBrowserTimezone(event.cookies)`). Running it after the call guarantees it
  sees post-rating due dates. It is deliberately *not* inside `rateNote`: that function is a
  scheduling primitive with no request scope, and pushing a timezone into it to serve a navbar
  counter is the wrong dependency direction. It gets its own transaction, not the caller's.
- `recordQuestCompletion` runs the same queue check itself, which is how a learner with an empty
  queue lights the day on the quest alone. The saved-day grant never consults it.

The queue check is one account-wide existence query mirroring `isReviewCardAvailable`. It compares
the stored strings directly instead of casting, because `serializeCard` always writes
`toISOString()` — fixed-width UTC, so lexicographic order is chronological — and because a cast is
what would let one malformed `fsrs_card` abort a quest-completion transaction:

```sql
select 1 from note where user_id = $1
  and ( fsrs_card->>'due' <= $nowIso
        or (fsrs_card->>'state' in ('1', '3') and fsrs_card->>'due' <= $learnAheadIso) )
  limit 1
```

This duplicates `isReviewCardAvailable` in SQL, so the two are pinned together by an equivalence
test (§6). One divergence is known and accepted: `deserializeCard` falls back to `new Date()` for a
malformed `due`, so TypeScript calls such a card *available* while the string comparison generally
does not. The gate may therefore light a day that a corrupt row would otherwise hold dark — the safe
direction, and preferable to crashing a completion.

### 2.4 Reaching the UI

`(app)/+layout.server.ts` returns the **record**, not a rendered view, and registers
`STREAK_DEPENDENCY` in `src/lib/load-dependencies.ts`. Shipping the record lets the client re-derive
at local midnight without a round trip and keeps SSR synchronous as the repo requires.

`src/routes/api/review/[noteId]/rate/+server.ts` adds `streak: StreakRecord | null` to its response
— the value `recordReviewObservation` returns, non-null only when something changed — and the study
UI calls `invalidate(STREAK_DEPENDENCY)` when it arrives. The transfer path does not need this: it
already runs `invalidateAll()` on completion.

## 3. The pure module

`src/lib/streak.ts` — no server imports, shared by loader, components, tests, and the harness:

```ts
export const STREAK_BANK_MAX = 3;

export type StreakRecord = {
	streakDays: number; throughDate: string | null; bank: number;
	progressDate: string | null; taskCount: number; reviewCleared: boolean; bankEarnedToday: number;
};
export type StreakStatus = "lit" | "pending" | "none";
export type StreakView = {
	days: number; bank: number; status: StreakStatus;
	taskCount: number; reviewCleared: boolean;
	questsToNextSavedDay: number | null;   // null when the badge is full
	savedDaysSpent: number;                // > 0 right after an absence the badge covered
};

export function localDay(at: Date | number, timeZone: string): string;
export function dayDelta(from: string, to: string): number;
export function savedDaysFor(taskCount: number): number;          // already capped
export function effectiveToday(record: StreakRecord | null, today: string): string;  // §1.4 clamp
export function settle(record: StreakRecord, today: string): { days: number; bank: number; spent: number; broken: boolean };
export function viewStreak(record: StreakRecord | null, today: string): StreakView;
export function applyQuestCompletion(record: StreakRecord | null, today: string, reviewQueueEmpty: boolean): StreakRecord;
export function applyReviewObservation(record: StreakRecord | null, today: string, queueEmpty: boolean): StreakRecord;
```

`today` is always an argument. Nothing in this module reads a clock, a cookie, or module-level
state, which is what lets the server, the client, the tests, and the harness's virtual dates all use
it without disagreeing about what day it is. The dev day offset (§5) is applied by the callers that
resolve `today`, never inside these functions.

Both transitions follow the same three steps:

1. **Settle** the carried streak through yesterday — spend saved days, or break.
2. **Update today's counters**, resetting them first if `progressDate` is not today: `taskCount + 1`,
   or `reviewCleared ||= queueEmpty` (never `=`, per §1.1). On a quest, grant
   `savedDaysFor(taskCount) - bankEarnedToday` saved days, clamped to the cap, and store the new
   `bankEarnedToday`. The grant is a delta against a stored value, so it cannot double-grant, and it
   never consults `reviewCleared`.
3. **Light the day** when `taskCount >= 1 && reviewCleared && throughDate !== today`: `streakDays +
   1`, `throughDate = today`.

`settle` on a record with `throughDate === null` returns `days: 0` and leaves `bank` untouched — the
"saved days without a streak" case from §1.3.

## 4. Presentation

`src/lib/components/streak/` — `StreakIndicator.svelte` (flame + number + badge + popover),
`StreakFlame.svelte` (the art and its states), `StreakDigits.svelte` (rolling number).

### 4.1 Placement

In the app navbar's right cluster, before the Trial pill, `mode === "app"` only — note that the
Trial pill is itself conditional (`Navbar.svelte:211`) and `sm:`-hidden, so the streak's own
breakpoint rules must be stated independently of it. Visible at every breakpoint; not folded into
the hamburger. One 44px tap target; the number uses tabular figures with a min-width wide enough for
three digits, so a four-digit streak is the only case that can move the language switcher.

The navbar is absent on session pages, which is fine: a quest completes into the feedback page,
which has it.

### 4.2 Visual states

Muted ember tones consistent with the warm-paper palette, added as tokens in `src/routes/layout.css`
(`--color-streak-flame` around `oklch(0.68 0.13 55)`, core `--color-accent-yellow`). No saturated
orange, no gradient wash.

- **lit** — filled flame with a soft warm drop-shadow, number in `--foreground`.
- **pending** — flame as a thin `--muted-foreground` outline at ~55% opacity, number muted; the
  badge stays in full accent colour with yesterday's value.
- **none** — same dimmed treatment, number `0`; the badge still shows if saved days were earned
  after the break.

The badge sits at the number's top-right as a small superscript pill reading `+1` … `+3`. At 0 the
slot collapses rather than rendering `+0`, and it appears with the earn animation when the first
saved day lands.

### 4.3 Popover

Click (or hover on pointer devices, using the existing origin-aware dropdown pattern) opens a small
paper card: streak length, today's two conditions with their state ("1 quest done", "12 cards left
in Español"), how many more quests reach the next saved day, and what saved days are for. With a
two-part daily rule this is not optional — it is how a learner finds out why today is still dark,
which language still owes reviews, and why the badge grew on a dark day.

The per-language card counts come from `/api/review/stats`, extended with an account-wide
`byLanguage` breakdown behind an opt-in `?byLanguage=1`, **fetched lazily when the popover first
opens** — not added to the layout
load, which would put an aggregate query on every app navigation to serve a panel most visits never
open. Until it resolves the popover shows the counts' row as a skeleton; the lit/dark state itself
never depends on that fetch, since `reviewCleared` is already in the record.

The breakdown is a detail view, not a live counter: ratings inside a transfer pass do not invalidate
the layout (only `completeTransfer` calls `invalidateAll()`), so a popover opened mid-pass can show
counts a few cards stale. Acceptable for a panel; the flame itself is driven by server truth.

### 4.4 Animations and when they fire

The trigger model matters more than the easing, because the flagship path is a **full page reload**:
session completion navigates with `window.location.href` (`session.svelte.ts:332`), so the indicator
mounts fresh with no previous value to diff against. A naive "diff the previous derived view"
approach plays nothing exactly where the learner most expects the flame to light.

So the component compares against a **last acknowledged view** persisted in `localStorage` holding
`{day, days, bank, status}`, not against its own previous render. The key is scoped by user id,
which means adding `id` to the user object `(app)/+layout.server.ts` returns — it currently exposes
only name, email, role and language fields, and keying on an email that can be changed in Profile
would silently replay every animation after a change.

- On mount and on every derived change, diff stored → current, play the transitions that explain the
  difference, then store the new value.
- First ever mount stores without animating.
- A full reload therefore animates correctly, and so does the translation path, which is a soft
  navigation with `invalidateAll()` (`translate/[id]/feedback/+page.svelte:428`) and needs no extra
  work.
- The streak-ended notification dedupes on the same stored value, so it fires **once per device**,
  not once per read of a derived `savedDaysSpent > 0`. Multi-device replay is accepted and arguably
  right.
- Local midnight: a timer to the next local midnight plus a `visibilitychange` re-check (a laptop
  that slept through midnight fires no timer) re-derives with the shared pure function and fades lit
  → pending. `displayClock` is only a request snapshot, so the client owns this tick.

Durations follow the repo's 220–350ms ease-out habit, no layout jump, each one reduced to an instant
state swap under `prefers-reduced-motion`:

| transition       | motion                                                                     |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| ignite (pending → lit) | outline cross-fades into fill over 320ms, flame scales 0.94 → 1.04 → 1, a warm glow blooms and decays over 600ms, three embers drift up 10px and fade |
| number +1        | 280ms digit roll: outgoing digit slides up and out, incoming from below; per-digit so 9 → 10 widens smoothly |
| saved day earned | badge pops 0.6 → 1.12 → 1 over 240ms with a single ring pulse; from 0 it fades in as the slot expands, and it plays on a dark day too |
| saved day spent  | badge rolls down, one ember travels badge → number as the number rolls up    |
| streak ends      | 420ms desaturation, number drifts down to 0; one `ActionNotification`, deduped as above, no alarming red |
| extinguish       | lit → pending fades over 300ms at the midnight tick                          |

### 4.5 Celebration contract

The richer completion effect on the feedback page is **not** in this cut, and no component is built
for it now. What is fixed now is the event contract the indicator emits, so the effect can be
designed later without touching workflow code:

```ts
type StreakEvent =
	| { kind: "lit"; days: number; bank: number }
	| { kind: "saved-day-earned"; bank: number }
	| { kind: "saved-day-spent"; days: number; spent: number }
	| { kind: "broken"; previousDays: number };
```

## 5. Development harness

Route `(app)/streak-lab`, `error(404)` unless `dev`. It renders inside the real app layout, so the
real navbar indicator is on screen while you drive it. Four panels:

1. **States.** Controls for days / saved days / status / quests today / reviews-cleared, written to
   a client override store (`src/lib/client/streak-preview.svelte.ts`) that `StreakIndicator`
   prefers over layout data; production never sets it and `dev` tree-shakes the import. A contact
   sheet shows every interesting state at once (lit, pending, none, 1–4 digits, badge 0–3).
2. **Animations.** Replay buttons for each `StreakEvent`, a reduced-motion toggle, and a 0.25×–1×
   speed control, played on the real navbar and on an enlarged inspector copy simultaneously. Every
   duration in these components is `calc(base * var(--streak-speed))`, which makes the speed control
   and the forced reduced-motion branch one dial: zero collapses each transition into the same
   instant state swap the media query produces, so the harness cannot drift from the real branch.
   Also a "clear acknowledged view" button, since the §4.4 `localStorage` entry is exactly what
   suppresses a replay.
3. **Simulation.** An editable day-by-day timeline — quests and a reviews-cleared flag per day — run
   through the pure functions into a table of date, events, streak, saved days, status. Presets:
   perfect week; one skipped day covered by the badge; three skipped days that break it; a ten-quest
   day; three quests with reviews left undone (dark day that earns `+1` and is then covered by it);
   quests after a break (saved days with a streak of 0). No database; this is the fast loop for
   tuning the rules.
4. **Live.** Dev-only form actions against the signed-in user's real row, through the production
   server functions: set state, simulate a quest completion, simulate a review observation, reset.
   Day travel uses a dev-only cookie (`libiamo-dev-streak-day-offset`). `src/lib/server/streak.ts`
   reads it through SvelteKit's `getRequestEvent()` and the app layout hands the same offset to the
   indicator, so *every* path travels — including a real conversation completed while the offset is
   set — without plumbing an offset argument through five call sites. It is compiled out of a
   production build and falls back to zero outside a request. `src/lib/streak.ts` itself never reads
   it (§3): the offset is applied where `today` is resolved.

## 6. Tests

Few and behavioural, per repo convention; fixed dates only.

- `test/lib/streak.test.ts` — saved-day thresholds at 1/3/6/10 and at 15 (capped, so the check
  constraint can never be violated); `settle` for gaps of 0/1/2 with and without enough saved days;
  the two-part lit rule; three quests without cleared reviews granting `+1` while the day stays
  dark, and that saved day then covering the day itself; saved days surviving a break on the day
  they were earned; repeated quest completions in one day granting exactly once; `reviewCleared`
  never retracting when a later observation finds cards due; the `effectiveToday` clamp against both
  `throughDate` and `progressDate`.
- `test/lib/server/streak.test.ts` — with a mocked `$lib/server/db`: `recordQuestCompletion` lights
  the day only when the queue check reports empty, and creates the row before locking it. Savepoint
  isolation is **not** tested here: a mocked db never enters a failed-transaction state, so such a
  test would assert nothing. It is an implementation constraint stated in §2.3, and the most a test
  can honestly check is that the helper opens a nested transaction.
- `test/lib/server/review-availability.test.ts` — the SQL gate predicate and `isReviewCardAvailable`
  agree over a fixture set of well-formed cards (due past/now/future, each state, both sides of the
  learn-ahead boundary). Malformed cards are excluded and their documented divergence (§2.3) is
  asserted instead. This is worth more than another rules test, because the duplication is the thing
  that will drift.
